# Post-test interview guide (Level 3)

Run immediately after the session — see `test-script.md`'s timing guidance. Semi-structured:
ask every numbered question, in order, but follow genuine tangents before pulling back. The
order is deliberate — it builds from concrete/specific to abstract/comparative so the litmus
question at the end is answered from earned context, not asked cold.

## Non-leading protocol — read before every session

The single most important rule in this document: **never say "ChatGPT," "moat," "differentiate,"
"competitor," or "better than AI" before the participant says a version of it first.** If you
say it first, any answer that echoes it back is worthless — you've told them the answer you want.

If a participant never mentions a comparison to a general AI assistant unprompted, that is
itself a finding, not a failure of the interview. Write it down as "no unprompted comparison
made" and move on. Only ask the direct comparison question (Q8) after every earlier, softer
opportunity for them to raise it themselves has passed.

## Section A — First reactions (open, unstructured)

1. "Walk me through what you just did, in your own words — what is this tool, and what did it
   do?"
2. "What was the moment, if any, where you felt like you understood what this was for?"
3. "Was there a moment where you didn't trust something it told you, or wanted to check it
   yourself?" *(Follow up specifically: "What made you want to check that one?")*

## Section B — The narrative itself

4. "Thinking about the final story it landed on — the Narrative Map screen — how would you
   describe, in one or two sentences, what that company's story is?" *(Compare their answer
   against what they wrote in `user-observation-sheet.md`'s task-completion row for this same
   ask, captured mid-session — did it change after reflection?)*
5. "Did that story feel like it was describing a company that's already arrived somewhere, or a
   company that's in the middle of becoming something? Which parts felt like each?"
6. "Was there anything in the story that felt like it might be more hope than fact? Which part?"

## Section C — Trust and evidence

7. "If you had to defend this narrative to a skeptical CEO or a skeptical journalist, what part
   would you feel confident defending, and what part would you want more proof for first?"

## Section D — The comparison (only if not already raised unprompted)

8. "Have you used a general AI assistant — ChatGPT, Claude, something like that — for anything
   like this before, developing a company narrative or positioning?"
   - If yes: "What was different about doing it that way versus this?" *(Let them answer fully
     before any follow-up. Do not suggest dimensions — if they don't mention evidence, honesty
     about maturity, or company-level framing on their own, don't prompt for it.)*
   - If no: "If you'd tried to do this same task by just asking a general AI assistant to write
     you a company narrative, what do you think would have been different about what you got
     back?"

## Section E — The litmus question, asked indirectly

9. "If you had to explain to a colleague why they should use this instead of just asking
   ChatGPT to write the same thing, what would you tell them? Or would you not be able to say?"

This is the moment the litmus quote from `README.md` either appears in the participant's own
words or it doesn't. **Do not lead them to it. Do not supply the sentence. Write down exactly
what they say, including hesitation, hedging, or "I'm not sure I would" — a hedged or absent
answer is a real, valid, important result.**

10. "Is there anything this tool told you that you now believe about the company that you didn't
    believe before you used it? What convinced you?"

## Section F — Closing

11. "What's the one thing that most got in the way of trusting or using this?"
12. "What's the one thing you'd keep exactly as is?"
13. "Anything else you noticed that we haven't talked about?"

## Immediately after the interview (moderator only, before the next session)

- Transcribe or paraphrase-with-exact-quotes the answers to Q9 and Q10 into
  `results-summary-template.md`'s moat-evidence log while the wording is fresh — do not
  paraphrase from memory later.
- Classify the Q9 answer into exactly one of: **(a) unprompted, specific, matches the litmus
  pattern; (b) unprompted but vague/generic; (c) only produced when directly asked (Q9 itself);
  (d) could not articulate a difference; (e) said there was no meaningful difference.** This
  classification is the single most important data point this framework produces.
